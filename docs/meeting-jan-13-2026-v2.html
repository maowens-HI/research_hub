Florida Private School Access and Demographics: County-Level Analysis (V2)

Published

January 13, 2026

Analysis Overview

This document presents three parallel analyses of the relationship between county-level demographics and private school access in Florida:

  1. 2000 Cross-Section (N=21 counties)
  2. 1990 Cross-Section (N=22 counties)
  3. 2000-1990 Difference (N=21 matched counties)

Key Features:

  • Geographic Unit: Counties
  • School Measure: Simple county-level counts (total schools in county)
  • School Levels: Elementary, middle, and high school combined
  • Weighting: School-age population (ages 5-17) from 1990 Census
Methodological Change from V1

This analysis uses simple county-level school counts rather than the 5-mile radius buffer approach used in V1. Instead of calculating average school exposure per Census block within a county, we now count the total number of private schools located within each county’s borders. This simplification addresses concerns about the complexity of the radius-based approach and provides a more straightforward interpretation: “How many private schools are in this county?”

The tradeoff is that we lose within-county variation in school access—a family in a rural part of a large county is treated the same as one near a cluster of schools. However, at the county level of analysis, this simpler measure may be more appropriate.

Key Findings Summary

Main Results

  1. Cross-sectional patterns: Counties with more private schools have significantly higher Black and Hispanic population shares. This holds for both 1990 and 2000, and for both religious and non-religious schools.

  2. Difference specification: Counties with more private schools experienced larger increases in minority population shares from 1990-2000, and larger decreases in white population share. This is the opposite of a “white flight” narrative.

  3. Interpretation: The positive relationship between private schools and minority shares likely reflects urbanization—growing metropolitan areas have both more demographic diversity and more private schools due to population density. The school-demographic relationship appears to be driven by geography rather than sorting.

  4. Multivariate controls: When controlling for multiple demographics simultaneously, Hispanic share remains a significant positive predictor of school counts in 1990, but most individual predictors become insignificant in the presence of controls, suggesting collinearity among the demographic measures.

Data Sources and Methods

Click to expand: Technical Details

Demographic Data:

  • Source: IPUMS USA Census 5% samples (1990 and 2000)
  • Variables: Income (household-level), race, ethnicity, education, age (person-level)
  • Geography: Counties identified in IPUMS (21-22 Florida counties)

School Data:

  • Source: Geocoded private school locations with county assignment
  • School Classifications:
    • Religious vs. Non-religious
    • Fine-grained: 14 original denomination categories
    • Collapsed: 8 broad categories

Sample Restrictions:

  • Florida only (statefip==12)
  • 21 IPUMS-identifiable counties for 2000
  • 22 IPUMS-identifiable counties for 1990
  • 21 matched counties for difference specification

Weighting:

  • All regressions weighted by school-age population (ages 5-17) from 1990 Census
  • Population data from NHGIS county-level age tables
